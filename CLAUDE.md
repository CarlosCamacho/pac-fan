# PAK FAN — working notes for the assistant

A maze-chase game for the TRS-80 Color Computer 1/2 in **Semigraphics 12** (64×96 elements,
8 colours), 6809 assembly, by Carlos A. Camacho (CAMCO). It is a fan game: never use the
Pac-Man name, Namco artwork or Namco music. The title says PAK FAN and CAMCO; the sounds are
original (`assets/audio/`, designed by the owner; the sound packs that came from the Namco ROM were refused). The owner writes in US English: use US spelling in replies and new text.

`README.md` describes the game. This file is how to work on it.

The owner, the other projects, the toolchain and the open threads from the earlier chat session
that built all this are in the handoff file, imported here:

@HANDOFF.md

## Build

```
LWASM=/path/to/lwasm ./build.sh      # gen.py -> tables.inc, then dist/PAKFAN.BIN, AUDIO.BIN, COPIER.BIN and the .DSK
```

- **LWTOOLS 4.24 or 4.25** (`build.sh` checks). 4.25 was checked to build a byte-identical
  `PAKFAN.BIN`. Neither is packaged for Ubuntu. The `stahta01/LWTools` mirror stops at 4.21;
  xroar-dev vendors `lwasm/lwtools-4.25.tar.gz` (build plain `make lwasm/lwasm` from it).
- `tables.inc` and `drawspr.inc` are **generated by gen.py**. Never edit them by hand.
- The build asserts the image ends below `$7400` (it was `$7000`; the BASIC stack, which the game
  uses under 20 bytes of, is at `$7F34`). At the last build it ended at `$6FED`: about 1,000 bytes
  are free. A level costs 201 (21 header, 120 tile data, 60 way-home map). Read the real figure from
  `ENDVARS` in `dist/pakfan.sym`; don't trust this line.

## Levels

- `levels/pacfan-project.json` is the owner's file from the maze editor
  (`editor/maze-editor.html`). **gen.py reads it and never writes it.** When the owner sends a
  new one, copy it over this file.
- `levels/order.json` is the **play order**, by level name. Level 1 stays first; the other
  39 were ranked easiest to hardest by the autopilot (below). Since 22 October 2026 the project
  file itself is in play order, renamed Level 1-40 by play position, with `wallColor` set to the
  colour the game uses, so `order.json` is just 1..40 (its comment maps the old names). A level added to the project
  must be added here too, or gen.py stops and names it.
- gen.py prints a line per level and **removes dots the player cannot reach**, warning about
  each. It also warns when the ghosts' exit is cut off from the player. As of the last
  build, no level has a warning. Dead ends are reported, but the owner uses them on
  purpose; they are not errors.
- Engine limits that gen.py enforces: 16×15 only; a house `H` with its door `D` directly
  above and an open tile above the door; wall colour set by play position in gen.py
  (`WALLCYCLE`: blue, red, cyan, magenta, orange, green, yellow; buff skipped because it reads
  as white), so the editor's `wallColor` and per-wall colours are ignored; `P` = player start (else the open tile nearest the bottom
  middle).

## How the engine works (where things live in pakfan.asm)

- Screens: `BUF0` `$0E00` and `BUF1` `$1A00`, double-buffered (SAM F 7/13), flipped at field
  sync; `BG` `$2600` is the background copy. A frame restores each sprite's 3×6-byte square
  from BG and redraws it. Code from `$3200`.
- `NEWLEVEL` reads the level header (`LVTAB`, `H_*` offsets) and calls `BUILDBG`, which
  unpacks 4-bit tile types and works out wall joins (`TCODE`) exactly as gen.py does.
- Ghosts: arcade targeting (`TARGET`, `CHOOSE`, `DIST2`, which is tunnel-aware on both
  axes), scatter/chase (`MODETICK`), house and door (`E_HOUSE`; `OPEN` lets only leaving
  ghosts and eyes through the door). **Eyes go home by a per-level shortest-path map**
  (`HOMEDIR`, built by gen.py), because straight-line targeting looped under a walled-off
  house.
- Ghost speed comes from the level number in `NEWLEVEL`: 15 for levels 1–8, plus 1 every 8
  levels, 19 from 33. The player is 16.
- Death: `DEATHFLASH` cycles the walls and the door (`MAP` 1 and 4) through the seven wall
  colours of `FLCOLS`, never buff, `FLFR` frames each (4; the owner chose to keep it), in the hidden buffer, and flips it
  in (recolouring the shown buffer wiped visibly down the screen). The door goes back to
  `DOORC` (magenta).
- Blue time: `NEWLEVEL` sets `FRTIME` = (21 - ghost speed) seconds, 6 s down to 2 s;
  `SCARE` loads it.
- CoCo 3: `START` checks the reset vector (`$8C1B` on a CoCo 3) and sets `COCO3`; on a
  CoCo 3 it writes `$FF9C` = 9 (SG12) and `QUIT` writes 15. Nothing is written to `$FF9C`
  on a CoCo 1/2.
- CoCo 3 speed: the sound loops are counted in cycles for 0.89 MHz, so a CoCo 3 left at high
  speed (`POKE 65497,0`) played the tune an octave up. After init, on a CoCo 3 only, `START`
  counts a 23-cycle loop between two field syncs (about 650 at 0.89 MHz, 1300 at 1.79;
  1000 or more is fast), writes `$FFD8` (normal speed) and sets `FASTF`; `QUIT` writes
  `$FFD9` to put high speed back. Never write `$FFD9` on a CoCo 1/2 (RAM refresh).
- CoCo 3 colours: SG colours come from palette registers 0-7. `START` saves BASIC's (`PALSAV`)
  and loads `PALCMP`, or `PALRGB` when register 1 reads `$36` (BASIC's `PALETTE RGB` set,
  ROM `$E664`; the composite start-up set is at `$C236`, register 1 = `$24`). `QUIT` puts
  `PALSAV` back. The two tables are the nearest of all 64 codes to xroar-dev's CoCo 2 colours
  (CIE Lab), measured with a probe that stepped the palette through every code. They match
  the emulator; a real composite set may differ. In xroar-dev on a CoCo 3, `xpc=` and `addr=` need `7` in front of the
  four hex digits, because the MMU maps logical `$0000-$FFFF` to physical `$70000-$7FFFF`.
- BREAK: `WAITVS` = `WAITV0` (wait for the field sync) then `CHKBRK`, so every wait in the
  game (frame loop, READY!, death flash, the pauses) polls BREAK, and the audio engine's blocking
  sounds (`A_PLAY`) poll it once a tick. Before this BREAK was dead for the 4.8 s tune and for the death sound, flash and
  READY! (22 of 29 test taps missed). `CHKBRK` clobbers A and B, so keep nothing live in them
  across `WAITVS` (callers push B). `QUIT` rests the DAC at `$82`.
- Score on screen: `ADDSCORE` saves the old score in `OLDS` (the old, unused `HISC` slot, which
  must follow `SCORE`: `HUDS` reads it as `3,y`) and calls `HUDS`, which redraws only the digits
  that changed (a dot changes one or two). `HUD` (new level, a life lost) sets `OLDS` to the
  complement of `SCORE`, so every digit counts as changed, runs `HUDS`, then draws `L nn` and
  the lives. Before, every dot redrew all ten glyphs into three buffers (about 8,000 cycles,
  over half a field), and a frame with that plus a ghost decision ran past the field 99-100%
  of the time. Long frames fell from 0.48% to 0.02%. The rest are frames that eat a ghost:
  `GULP` is a blocking sound on purpose (the arcade pauses there too).
- BASIC after BREAK: BASIC's program area starts at `TXTTAB` (`$0019`, `$2601` with Disk BASIC)
  and the game's screens and background cover it. BASIC's pointers survive, but the three bytes
  that make an empty program (a zero before it, a zero link at its start) were overwritten, so
  `LIST` showed garbage and a typed program gave `?SN ERROR` (CoCo 2) or wrong output (CoCo 3);
  only immediate commands like `PRINT` worked. `QUIT` now writes them back as `NEW` leaves them
  and points `VARTAB`/`ARYTAB`/`ARYEND` just after, when `TXTTAB` is at or above `BUF0` (a
  program stored below `$0E00` was not touched). The game changes nothing else of BASIC's: a
  before/after memory comparison showed only the game's own range `$0E00-$6F13` differing.
- Labels `LCLEAR`, `GOVER`, `MGREV`, `MGOUT`, `STWD`, `STWU`, `STARTMUS` are test hooks for emulator
  traps. Keep them.

## The title menu (pakfan.asm: MENUDRAW, MENUIN) and the ABOUT story

- Title: `PAK FAN BY CAMCO` on top, the chase, then four rows (element rows 44, 51, 58, 65):
  `PLAY`, `CONTROL:KEYBOARD|JOYSTICK`, `SOUND:DAC|ORCH90|OFF`, `ABOUT`, and at the bottom
  `PROGRAMMER:` / `CARLOS CAMACHO` / `(C) 1984 CAMCO` (a row is 16 glyphs of 4 elements, so the
  programmer line is two). The selected row is white, the others yellow.
- Keys: UP/DOWN pick a row; LEFT/RIGHT change it (CONTROL and SOUND cycle, wrapping
  both ways; PLAY has no level choice any more: every game starts at level 1); SPACE, ENTER and the right fire button do the row (PLAY starts, ABOUT shows the story,
  CONTROL/SOUND step on). Presses are edges (`MPREV`). The stick also drives the menu when CONTROL is JOYSTICK.
- Joystick (`JOYDIR`, `JOYXY`, `JOYAX`): the right stick, X with CA2 = 0 and Y with CA2 = 1, by successive
  approximation with the DAC (comparator = `$FF00` bit 7, 1 = stick above the DAC), 20/44 thresholds on
  0-63. A stick that is not there reads 0,0 (hard left and up), so nothing counts until it has been seen
  near the middle (`JSEEN`, cleared when CONTROL changes). In the game `READKEYS` adds the stick's
  directions to `E_WANT` when `OPTCTL` is 1; the arrow keys always work. The stick leaves the DAC at
  rest and the mux on the DAC, as the sound engine needs.
- SOUND: `SNDLIST` is the choices this build can make (ids into `SNDTX`: 0 DAC, 1 GMC, 2 SSC, 3 ORCH90,
  4 SIDX, 5 OFF); GMC, SSC and SIDX are in the menu's design but have no driver yet. `SNDSET` applies a
  choice: `HAVEAUD` (the wrappers and `WAITV0` test it) is `ENGINE` and not OFF, and the engine's
  `A_SETMODE` ($8010) takes 0 for the DAC, 1 for Orchestra-90.
- Orchestra-90: a second sample loop (`SLO`) writes `$FF7A` and `$FF7B` (both DACs, `$80` centre),
  116 cycles a sample, and `TICK` stretches every phase step by 116/111, so the pitch stays. Tested in
  xroar-dev with `-cart mpi -mpi-load-cart rsdos -mpi-load-cart orch90`: 246 of 246 start-music
  ticks right.
- ABOUT (`A_ABOUT`, engine, $800D; A = 1 on a CoCo 3): SG24 at `$0E00` (the two screen buffers), the
  story in white 3x5 glyphs two scanlines tall, 16 to a line and 14 lines a page, `PAGE n/N` in yellow
  under it; any key or fire turns the page (it waits for the keys to be up first); after the last page it
  returns and `NEWGAME` redraws the title. The story is `assets/about/story.txt` (capitals; paragraphs
  with a blank line between), made into pages by `tools/mkabout.py` (run by `build.sh`). The glyphs are
  `font3x5.py`, shared with `gen.py`. Without the engine (32K, or no sound pack) ABOUT does nothing.
- Every move plays the bounce (sound 9). Checks: `menu` (CONTROL, SOUND wrapping, ABOUT through to PLAY)
  and `joy` (a scripted stick, `-joy-right script`, moves the menu and the player).

## Audio engine (audio.asm, assets/audio/, tools/mksynth.py)

- The game runs in the normal memory map except for its sounds: `audio.asm` is assembled at `$8000`
  and the game reaches it by switching the SAM to **all-RAM mode** (`sta $FFDF`) in `START`; `QUIT`
  switches back (`sta $FFDE`) so BASIC is there again. `START` reads the first 4 bytes at `$8000`
  (`PFA` + 1); if they are not there (32K machine, or the game `LOADM`ed alone) it goes back to
  ROM mode and `HAVEAUD` stays 0: every sound call returns at once, the game is silent. The game never
  calls a ROM and runs with interrupts off, so the missing vectors do not matter (RESET resets the SAM).
- A `LOADM` cannot write above `$8000` (in ROM mode there is no RAM there to write), so `RUN"PAKFAN"`
  loads the engine in 12 pieces (`AUD01-12.BIN`, each with `copier.asm`, 2000 bytes at `$3200`) and `EXEC`s
  the copier after each, which goes to all-RAM mode, copies up and comes back. The game's own pieces
  load after, over the staging area. `TOGGLE.BIN` is for a test. On a CoCo 3, BASIC copies its ROM into the upper RAM at start-up, so a test
  puts the engine in after that (`-trap xpc=START -trap-load ... addr=0x78000`); on a CoCo 1/2
  `-load-ram dist/AUDIO.RAW,addr=0x8000` is enough. Both are done by `tools/emu.sh` and `checks.sh`.
- Engine: jump table at `$8004` (init), `$8007` (`A_SLICE`), `$800A` (`A_PLAY`: id in A), `$800D` (`A_ABOUT`), `$8010` (`A_SETMODE`). Ids 0-4 play
  to the end and return C=1 on BREAK (start music, intermission, credit, death, ghost eaten);
  5-9 start an effect that plays in idle time (extra life, two dot chirps, bonus, bounce); 10-16 start a
  loop under everything (five sirens, frightened, eyes); `$FF` stops the loop, `$FE` everything.
  `SNDB`/`SNDS` in `pakfan.asm` are the wrappers (`SNDB` leaves the game on BREAK).
- `WAITV0` (so every wait in the game) calls `A_SLICE` instead of idling: it steps one **tick** (a video
  field) of the playing streams, then makes samples until the field sync, 111 cycles each (8,062 Hz,
  measured). A blocking sound is the same slices in a loop. Inside the idle time a sound is chopped at
  the frame rate (only the idle part of each field is sound), the same as the old wa-ka was.
- A tick is three voices of 3 bytes: phase step (16 bits) and `wave << 4 | volume`; the tables
  (144 x 32 signed bytes) are built by `tools/mksynth.py` from `assets/audio/build_audio.py` (the
  owner's designs: it records each design's notes, sweeps and bursts and samples them once a tick).
  Voices: the long sounds use all three, effects voices 1 and 2, the loops 0 and 1, so an effect
  replaces the loop's voice 1 while it sounds. Pitch comes from `LOOP_CYCLES` in `mksynth.py`;
  `tools/checks.sh tune` checks it.
- The copier is at `$7000`, above the game.
- Ambient: `AMBIENT` (once a frame) picks eyes, else frightened, else the siren by dots eaten
  (`DOTSTEP` = a fifth of the maze's dots). `CURAMB` is the loop now playing.
- Extra life: `ADDSCORE` adds one (to 9) when the score first reaches 10,000.
- The numbers: 8,062 Hz is 111 cycles: with 114 the pitch was 2% sharp in xroar-dev. Nothing has been
  heard on real hardware (the emulator's CPU timing is the reference).
- To change a sound: edit `assets/audio/build_audio.py`, `python3 tools/mksynth.py` (needs numpy),
  `./build.sh`. `tools/audiocheck.py` compares a recording with a stream.

## Testing — the emulator is xroar-dev

Install it: <https://github.com/milliluk/xroar-dev>. The CoCo 2 ROMs (`bas13.rom`,
`extbas11.rom`, `disk11.rom`) are not in this repo; the owner must supply them.

```
export XROAR=/path/to/xroar-dev ROMS=/path/to/roms
tools/checkbg.sh          # every level: engine-built background == gen.py's, byte for byte
tools/checks.sh           # robustness: BREAK everywhere, leftover-RAM fuzz, CoCo 3 speed, lap wrap, frame budget
tools/botgames.sh 10      # autopilot: 10 games per level (about 4 minutes on 4 cores)
python3 tools/rank.py     # difficulty ranking; --write rewrites levels/order.json
python3 tools/botdiff.py OLD_DIR OLD.sym NEW_DIR NEW.sym   # did an edit change any game?
```

- `tools/checks.sh` (run after `./build.sh`; about two minutes; each line prints PASS or FAIL):
  `break` taps BREAK at 29 moments and leaves mid-tune, mid-flash and mid-READY! on a CoCo 2
  and a CoCo 3, then expects BASIC and `EXEC` to work; `basic` types a program after BREAK and
  expects it to list and run; `fuzz` fills the variable area with
  different patterns at `START` and expects identical screens and state (title and autopilot
  play, CoCo 2 and 3); `speed` (CoCo 3, needs `coco3.rom`) starts at high speed and checks
  normal speed is left alone and high speed comes back; `tune` records the speaker output of
  the start music and checks (`tools/audiocheck.py`) that every voice of every tick has its pitch in the
  spectrum (CoCo 2, CoCo 3, and a CoCo 3 left at high speed; needs numpy); `sound` expects dot-eating to be audible in 8 s
  of autopilot play; `lap` forces a clear of level 40;
  `title` presses UP/DOWN (wrapping both ways) and SPACE and expects that level; `score` plays
  45 s (and a run that starts at 999,990) and expects the score drawn in BG and both screens
  to equal the score, digit for digit; `eyes` follows the loaded way-home map from every tile
  of every level and expects the house tile, never through a wall; `overrun` plays all 40
  levels with random arrow keys (`tools/keyplay.py`) and fails if more than 0.05% of frames
  run past one video field (not counting frames that eat a ghost); `protect` runs the game and
  the autopilot build on all 40 levels under `-protect-mode` and fails on a write into code, a
  jump into data, or the stack more than 64 bytes below where BASIC left it. Each check was
  shown to FAIL on a build with the bug it guards against. `SYM=`, `DSK=`, `SRC=`, `LST=` point
  it at another build.
- **Audio** can be recorded: `-audio-record-source mix` is what reaches the speaker (after the
  PIA's sound-source select and enable bits, so it catches a game that runs but is silent);
  `dac` is the raw DAC. `-trap-audio-start FILE` / `-trap-audio-stop` bracket a window.
  `tools/audiocheck.py WAV STREAM.bin` compares a recording with a sound's stream. Start music:
  246 of 246 ticks right on a CoCo 2, a CoCo 3 and a CoCo 3 at high speed.
- **Protect mode** (`-protect-mode`) works only on the CoCo 3 memory path, which runs this
  code unchanged, so the `protect` check runs there (physical = `$70000` + logical).
  `tools/mksym.py LISTING SYM OUT BASE` makes its code map from the assembler listing:
  instruction bytes are code, `fcb`/`fdb` tables are data, `rmb` (variables, buffers, stack) is
  neither. Regenerate the map after every build. The listing indents source by 9 columns. The
  buffers `$0E00-$31FF` are exempted because `COPY3K` uses `S` as a pointer into them. Measured
  with the stack floor: the game uses under 20 bytes of stack, with 3 KB of room.
- **Before any edit that should not change gameplay** (sound, input, drawing, CoCo 3),
  save `botgames.sh 10` output and its `dist/bot/pakfan.sym`, edit, run again and compare with
  `tools/botdiff.py`: the autopilot is deterministic, so every game must end identically
  (400 of 400 did for the BREAK fix, the speed guard and the score redraw). The game state depends on
  frames, not cycles, so even a change in how long a frame takes must not alter a game.
  `botdiff` leaves out `FRAMES`, the animation phase: it counts title frames, so any start-up
  edit shifts it by a frame in every game alike.
- `-DOVR=1` adds a frame-overrun counter to the frame loop (`OVRCHK`): frames that ran past
  the next field sync, tabulated by what the frame did (ghost decisions, score redraws, a ghost
  eaten, a pill, a mode switch). Test only, never shipped; the test builds may end up to `$7800`.
  **Measure the real build, not `-DBOT=1`**: the autopilot's path-finding is not in the game.
  **Zero test counters at `START`**: RAM is not zero, and an uncleared counter once made the
  long-frame rate look 15 times worse (7% instead of 0.5%), which was reported before it was
  caught.
- `-DBOT=1` builds an **autopilot** into `READKEYS` (breadth-first path to the nearest dot,
  avoiding live ghosts). It is test-only and never shipped.
- xroar-dev traps: `xpc=` takes a physical address (5 hex digits). **`-trap-range N` means
  "from hit N on"; use `N-N` for a single hit.** `seconds=` is emulated time. A screenshot
  name may or may not get a digit appended, so glob for it.
- Headless runs can't be heard. Sound pitch comes from the engine's sample loop (111 cycles,
  `LOOP_CYCLES` in `tools/mksynth.py` must match it).
- **Nothing has run on real hardware yet.** Say so when reporting results.

## Traps already hit in this code

- `LDA B,X` is a **signed** offset: tile numbers 128–239 index backwards. Use `ABX`. (This
  hit the autopilot; the game's own `B,X` uses index tables of 21 entries or fewer.)
- `OPEN` clobbers A, B and X, so keep loop counters in memory (`CHN`), not in B. That once
  hung frightened ghosts in dead ends.
- After `ABX`, X has moved. Reload it before indexing again.
- xroar-dev's CoCo 3 runs BASIC about 1.7 times faster after any `$FFDF`/`$FFDE` toggle (it does
  not on a CoCo 2); `checks.sh speed` toggles once (`TOGGLE.BIN`) before its first measurement.
- A CoCo 3 copies BASIC's ROM into the upper RAM at start-up, so anything put there for a test must
  go in after boot.
- The audio engine keeps its variables in the page `$FD00` (not `$FE00`: a CoCo 3 keeps its interrupt vectors there), with DP set to `$FD` while it runs
  (it restores DP). It saves A, X, Y, U; nothing else in the game needs to.

## Loader and logo

- `./build.sh` ends with `tools/mkpak.py`, which writes `dist/PAKFAN.BAS` (plain-text BASIC loader),
  `PAKLOGO.BIN` (the SG24 loading screen from `assets/pakfan-logo.bin`, 6144 bytes at `$0E00-$25FF`),
  `AUD01..AUD12.BIN` (the audio engine, 2000 bytes a file, each with `copier.asm`) and
  `PAK01..PAK10.BIN` (the game in ten pieces) next to the unchanged `PAKFAN.BIN`, and builds
  `PAKFAN.DSK` with all of them (and `TOGGLE.BIN` for a test; `dist/AUDIO.RAW` for `-load-ram`).
  `RUN"PAKFAN"` shows the logo, loads the engine (a `LOADM` and an `EXEC` of the copier for each file),
  then the game, filling a progress bar after each of the 22 files, then EXECs the game; `START`
  switches to SG12 and `FILL3` clears the logo into the title.
  `LOADM"PAKFAN":EXEC` still works, but the game has no sound without the engine. Only BASIC's LOADM
  is used (floppy, CoCoSDC, DriveWire). The
  loader checks that BASIC's program starts at `$2601` (the screen ends at `$25FF`) and otherwise
  loads plainly. On a CoCo 3 it sets `$FF9C` = 10 (SG24) and BASIC's palette registers to the game's
  colors, and puts BASIC's palette back before EXEC. Loading takes about 35 s of emulated time.
- SG24 bytes are `1 CCC L R L R`: a CoCo 1/2 reads bits 3-2 on the upper 6 scanlines of its
  12-line character row and bits 1-0 on the lower 6. The Studio's export leaves bits 1-0 clear, which
  shows as black stripes on a CoCo 1/2 (the CoCo 3 shows it right), so `mklogo.py` repeats the pair.
- `tools/mklogo.py ART OUT.BIN [PREVIEW.PNG] [--save-art ART.PNG]` (needs numpy and Pillow) makes the
  screen from a Studio `.asm` or the art PNG. The art must be at most 64 cells wide; it is never scaled.
  Layout constants are in `tools/logolayout.py`. The game code is unchanged (217 bytes free).
- Checked in xroar-dev on a CoCo 2 and a CoCo 3 by screenshots (logo, bar filling, title). Not done: a
  `loader` check in `tools/checks.sh`, and real hardware. On a CoCo 3, `START` runs its speed test
  (two or three fields) after switching to SG12 and before `FILL3`, so the logo shows stretched for
  about 50 ms before the title. BREAK while loading leaves the SG24 screen (RESET, or type `SCREEN 0,0` blind).
